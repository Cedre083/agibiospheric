01 Energy

  • Availability : Accessible energy conditions every action.
  • Density : The denser an energy source, the more valuable it is.
  • Conversion : Every transformation entails a loss.
  • Transport : Moving energy costs energy.
  • Storage : Storing energy is difficult, slow, and costly.
  • Dissipation : Nothing is perfectly conserved.
  • Efficiency : Systems have physical limits.
  • Inertia : Energy transitions are slow.
  • Depletion : Stocks always eventually decline.
  • Real Cost : The cheapest energy is the energy not consumed.

02 Freshwater

  • Availability : Usable freshwater is minuscule.
  • Quality : Clean water is rarer than water itself.
  • Variability : Rainfall patterns shift; cycles become distorted.
  • Natural Storage : Soils, aquifers, glaciers: fragile reservoirs.
  • Pollution : Polluted water is water lost.
  • Competition : Agriculture, industry, cities: constant trade-offs.
  • Evaporation : Heat accelerates losses.
  • Droughts : They are becoming longer and more frequent.
  • Floods : Too much water can mean no drinking water at all.
  • Water Justice : Water distributes power.

03 Living Soils

  • Microbiomes : Soil is a living organism.
  • Fertility : Fertility is a process, not a stock.
  • Slowness : One centimeter of soil takes centuries to form.
  • Erosion : Loss is fast; rebuilding is slow.
  • Structure : Roots, pores, aggregates: a fragile architecture.
  • Humus : Soil carbon is vital.
  • Fungal Networks : The invisible highways of living systems.
  • Compaction : Weight destroys soil respiration.
  • Pollution : Contaminated soils lose their functions.
  • Desertification : Dead soil becomes dust.

04 Biodiversity

  • Networks : Living systems function through relationships.
  • Functions : Each species performs a unique function.
  • Resilience : Diversity absorbs shocks.
  • Niches : Ecological niches are limited.
  • Pollination : A critical process for plants.
  • Decomposers : They close the cycles.
  • Symbioses : Vital cooperations.
  • Fragmentation : Habitats become fragmented.
  • Extinctions : They are accelerating.
  • Lost Functions : A vanished species means a lost relationship.

  • Trajectories : Warming is a trajectory, not a fixed endpoint.

  • Glaciers : Their retreat is altering rivers.
  • Rainfall : It is becoming unpredictable.
  • Droughts : They are intensifying.

06 Ocean

  • Acidification : It weakens marine ecosystems.
  • Currents : They are shifting.
  • Upwellings : Their weakening reduces productivity.
  • Dead Zones : They are expanding.
  • Reefs : Bleaching is accelerating.
  • Migration : Species are relocating.
  • Oxygen : Deoxygenation is progressing.

  • Extraction : It destroys, consumes, and displaces.
  • Geology : Geological cycles are slow.
  • Metals : Some are becoming critical.
  • Rare Earths : Concentrated, difficult to extract.
  • Recycling : Partial, costly, limited.
  • Waste : They are accumulating.
  • Energy Cost : Matter costs energy.
