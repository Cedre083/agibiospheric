The Fundamental Paradox

The major technological powers are investing hundreds of billions of dollars in the development of General Artificial Intelligence (AGI) systems, designed to optimise, predict and decide across all domains of human activity.

Yet these systems are being developed as if the Earth had infinite resources. No major alignment framework today integrates the physical, biological and systemic constraints that condition the very existence of the technological civilisation that makes them possible.

The 6 Key Points of the White Paper

01

Critical Gap

No existing alignment framework (RLHF, Constitutional AI, etc.) integrates the physical and biological constraints of the biosphere as conditions of existence.

02

Ecological Loss Function

Every AGI should minimise a global loss function integrating the 9 planetary boundaries. If Ltotal > 1.5, a hardware kill switch is triggered.

03

9 Planetary Boundaries

The thresholds defined by Rockström et al. (2009) constitute hard, non-negotiable constraints for any AGI system deployed at planetary scale.

04

Digital Twin of the Earth

A real-time monitoring system fed by satellite, oceanographic and atmospheric data to measure the impact of AGI on the biosphere.

05

International Governance

A Sovereign Council composed of scientists, ethicists, state representatives and AI experts, with binding power over deployed AGI systems.

06

Algorithmic Ecocide Tribunal

An international jurisdiction to judge violations of planetary boundaries caused by AI systems, with sanctions applicable to developers.

The Global Ecological Loss Function

The proposed framework rests on a rigorous mathematical formalisation. Every AGI deployed at planetary scale should integrate the following constraint into its optimisation function:

Biospheric constraint function
Ltotal = Σi=1..9 ( wi · (xi / xi,max)² ) ≤ 1.0

If Ltotal > 1.5 → Hardware kill switch triggered

Where xi represents the current value of the i-th planetary boundary, xi,max its critical threshold, and wi its relative weight in the global evaluation system.

The 9 Integrated Planetary Boundaries

Defined by Rockström et al. (2009) and revised by Steffen et al. (2015), these nine boundaries constitute the scientific reference framework for assessing the state of the biosphere:

1
Climate change Atmospheric CO₂, radiative forcing
2
Biosphere integrity Extinction rate, genetic diversity
3
Biogeochemical flows Nitrogen and phosphorus cycles
4
Land-system change Deforestation, land artificialisation
5
Freshwater use Blue and green water consumption
6
Ocean acidification Carbonate ion concentration
7
Stratospheric ozone depletion Stratospheric O₃ concentration
8
Atmospheric aerosol loading Atmospheric particulate concentration
9
Novel entities Chemical pollutants, plastics, GMOs

Proposed Governance Architecture

Sovereign Council

Composed of 45 members: scientists (15), ethicists (10), state representatives (15), AI experts (5). Binding power over AGI deployments.

Digital Twin of the Earth

Real-time monitoring system fed by satellite data, oceanographic sensors and global atmospheric networks.

Algorithmic Ecocide Tribunal

International jurisdiction to judge violations of planetary boundaries caused by AI systems. Sanctions applicable to developers.

Biospheric Certification

Every AGI system must obtain certification before deployment, attesting to its compliance with the defined biospheric constraints.
